## Identity anchor and ambiguity

The raw value is `曹薰铉` (simplified Chinese). Korean Baduk Association records identify the professional as `조훈현`, Hanja `曺薰鉉`, born 1953-03-10, a Korean Go professional and 9-dan; an official recent association report also refers to him as `조훈현 9단` and pairs him with his student Lee Chang-ho. Sources in other languages independently describe the same South Korean Go player, born in Mokpo in 1953, who taught Lee Chang-ho. The raw first character `曹` differs from the player’s Hanja `曺`; Chinese-language sources commonly write `曹薰铉`. This spelling difference alone does not establish or refute identity. The 9-dan figure is professional Go rank, not a title, tournament result, or name component.

Identity sources (reviewed 2026-10-02): [Korean Baduk Association game archive search for 조훈현](https://www.baduk.or.kr/gibo/gibo_in.asp?foreignKey=%EC%A1%B0%ED%9B%88%ED%98%84), observed `ko`, exact result fields `조훈현`; [Korean Baduk Association 2026 report](https://baduk.or.kr/news/report_view.asp?news_no=5807), observed `ko`, `조훈현 9단` and `이창호 9단`; [English Wikipedia, Cho Hun-hyun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cho_Hun-hyun), observed `en`, “Cho Hunhyun (; born 10 March 1953) is a South Korean professional Go player”; [Korea.net French-language report](https://french.korea.net/NewsFocus/HonoraryReporters/view?articleId=208964), observed `fr`, calls Lee Chang-ho the pupil of “Cho Hunhyun” and identifies him as a famous Korean Go player. The association archive is primary name/rank corroboration; the international sources and pupil relationship support cross-language identity discrimination.
