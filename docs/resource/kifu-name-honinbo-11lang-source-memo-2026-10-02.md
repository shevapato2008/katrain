## Identity and normalization boundary

The target is the Japanese professional Honinbo title tournament/series, Japanese name 本因坊戦. It is distinct from (a) an individual player holding the Honinbo title or adopting an honorary Honinbo name, (b) the historic Honinbo Go house, and (c) Women's/Amateur/Junior/local Honinbo tournaments. The official Nihon Ki-in archive calls the event “Honinbo Title,” labels the event field “Tournament name,” identifies sponsors, and presents successive numbered editions. Its Japanese pages state 棋戦名称 本因坊戦 and record numbered periods; the CWI archive similarly separates tournament title games and the 1st edition's tournament from the eventual title holder. These establish that an English leading ordinal belongs to an edition attribute while the residual name identifies the recurring series.

The prior source matrix lists the affected corpus strings as `26th` through `31st Honinbo` and already treats the ordinal as an edition. This research follows that boundary and the supplied 1,617-game group count; it does not recompute production membership. Source histories use Japanese 期/期数, while the English game corpus uses ordinal words, so retain the ordinal as source edition metadata and preserve any round/stage separately.

## Cautions and next review state

- These are sourced candidates, not a translation decision. `Honinbo` is a proper name and varies by transliteration/diacritics; preserve the exact locally witnessed spelling if a language-specific display label is later approved.
- Japanese `本因坊戦` and Korean `혼인보센` are series forms; `本因坊` / `혼인보` alone can instead mean the house, title, or titleholder. Chinese pages explicitly separate the tournament from the champion's 本因坊 title. Avoid using holder labels such as `Honinbo Shusaku` or honorary numbered names as event-series names.
- `本因坊戰` and `本因坊战` are orthographic script variants for the same Japanese event, not separate series. The Taiwanese row was witnessed in the Wikipedia Taiwan Traditional variant; Chinese Traditional text can still contain Japanese kanji where the spelling is shared.
- The current event format and edition numbering changed in later years. Do not infer an edition from calendar year; preserve the edition number stated in source game metadata.
