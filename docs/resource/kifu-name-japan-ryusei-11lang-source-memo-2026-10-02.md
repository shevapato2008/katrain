# Japanese Ryusei identity and 11-language source memo (2026-10-02)

Read-only research for the frozen event core `日本龙星战`: 13 Chinese event-label variants across the current v5 event groups, about 1,193 games. This memo separates source-attested labels from translation suggestions; it does not approve names or alter event data. The [independent follow-up](kifu-name-japan-ryusei-uncertain-sol-2026-10-02.md) supplies stronger Korean, German, and Chinese evidence and corrects weak initial leads.

## Identity finding

The intended series is Japan's domestic professional televised Ryusei tournament, Japanese official title **竜星戦**. Nihon Ki-in's archive records the domestic editions continuously: 1st edition in 1991 and, for the corpus slice at hand, 13th edition in 2004. Its current page identifies the event, organizer(s), format, and founding year: the 35th edition is listed as founded in 1990 and organized by Igo & Shogi Channel, Inc. and Nihon Ki-in; the detailed 9th-edition page likewise names the series 竜星戦. Sources: [Nihon Ki-in historical results](https://www.nihonkiin.or.jp/match/ryusei/archive.html), [Nihon Ki-in 9th edition](https://www.nihonkiin.or.jp/match/ryusei/009.htm).

There is a dating nuance: the official archive's edition-results table labels edition 1 as 1991, while the event profile says it was founded in 1990. Treat 1990 as establishment/founding and 1991 as first recorded edition/game year, rather than forcing them into one date. The currently frozen records `日本第1期`–`日本第13期龙星战` therefore cover editions 1–13 / recorded years 1991–2004, with a skipped edition-year gap in the official chronology (no 5th-edition row for 1995; 5th edition is 1996). The endpoints should be validated against individual SGF dates if a date-level boundary is needed.

The Japanese organizer is **株式会社囲碁将棋チャンネル** (Igo & Shogi Channel, Inc.; historical 9th-edition page says Nihon Ki-in as organizer and the channel as sponsor; current editions list both as organizers). Do not state that Nihon Ki-in alone is the organizer for every edition.

Identity boundary: `中国龙星战` is a separate Chinese domestic competition. Igo & Shogi Channel's official page calls it 中国竜星戦, says it was founded in 2006 and is hosted by 中国棋院 with the channel as co-organizer: [official China Ryusei page](https://www.igoshogi.net/igo/china_ryusei/). Korean 竜星戦/류성전 is also a separately discussed domestic series (the channel's 2017 minutes refer to plans for a 韓国竜星戦). Japan–China champion matches are a separate international event, explicitly named 日中竜星戦 by Nihon Ki-in and distinct from a Japan edition: [official Japan–China program](https://www.igoshogi.net/igo/special/japanchina_ryusei2014/nichu_ryuseisen2014.pdf). The 2019 Japan–China–Korea champions event is likewise not the domestic numbered series. Qualifier/preliminary/main-tournament labels within an edition are stages of the Japanese series, not independent tournaments; the official event page separates 本戦 and 予選A・B.

## Open issues / handoff

- The independent Sol follow-up resolved the Korean and German source wording and found direct Chinese reporting. Spanish evidence remains weak; Turkish and Ukrainian remain generated-only suggestions.
- Chinese Simplified and Traditional suggestions preserve the country qualifier. The canonical event entity should retain a Japan distinction even if a UI display name later omits it in Japanese.
- Japanese primary material's founding year 1990 and first recorded edition year 1991 are both correct in their respective contexts; do not normalize away the distinction.
- `竜星戦` is an umbrella series that includes preliminary qualifiers and the final tournament. SGF records titled `第N期日本龙星战预选` or equivalent can map to the series with a stage component, but they should not be conflated with Chinese/Korean Ryusei competitions or the Japan–China match.

No database writes, candidate approvals, code changes, or commits were made.
