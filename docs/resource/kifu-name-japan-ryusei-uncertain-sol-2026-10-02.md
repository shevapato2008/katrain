# Japanese 竜星戦: unresolved locale follow-up (2026-10-02)

Scope: follow-up to [the 11-language source memo](kifu-name-japan-ryusei-11lang-source-memo-2026-10-02.md) for `ko`, `de`, `es`, `tr`, and `ua`, with direct checks of `cn` and `tw`. This is source research and a naming recommendation, not a canonical-name approval or a database change.

## Event identity used for every check

The target is Japan's domestic professional Go tournament **竜星戦**, not China's domestic 龙星战, Korea's domestic 용성전, a champions match between the countries, 新竜星戦, or an unrelated title in another game. The [Nihon Ki-in archive](https://www.nihonkiin.or.jp/match/ryusei/archive.html) anchors the numbered Japanese series: editions 1–13 run from 1991 to 2004. The event profile describes a 1990 founding; that is compatible with the first listed edition in 1991. Names below were checked against the domestic event or an identifiable Japanese edition, player, and result. The [official Chinese Ryusei page](https://www.igoshogi.net/igo/china_ryusei/) and [Japan–China champions program](https://www.igoshogi.net/igo/special/japanchina_ryusei2014/nichu_ryuseisen2014.pdf) establish separate identities.

## Decision boundary

- Korean can move from pending to source-attested **일본 용성전**. German can retain source-attested **Ryusei**. Spanish has an attested Latin-script headword **Ryusei**, while its Japanese qualifier remains editorial. Turkish and Ukrainian remain generated only.
- `Ryusei Cup` in the German magazine refers to the Japanese competition because it sits in the Japan results list and names the known 2019 finalists. `Ryongsang Cup (Ryusei)` in the same magazine's 2018 coverage is a Korean event and must not be used as Japanese-title evidence.
- A source's publication or page update year is not the tournament's founding year. Keep **founded 1990** separate from **first recorded edition 1991**, and treat editions 1–13 as the archive's 1991–2004 span rather than a continuous year-to-edition formula.
- The Traditional Chinese Wikipedia article gives 1992 for edition 1 and shifts several early edition years relative to the Nihon Ki-in archive. It is useful for name/identity attestation, but the Japanese organizer's archive controls chronology. The Korean Wikipedia article likewise reflects an older organizer/sponsor description, so its wording does not override the current Japanese profile's organizer details.

Research was bounded to direct Japanese identity sources, locale-specific Go searches, and targeted source reads. Missing native attestations above mean none were found in this search, not that no such usage exists. No event records, approvals, code, or commits were changed.
