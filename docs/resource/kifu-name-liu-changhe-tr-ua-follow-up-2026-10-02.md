# 刘昌赫 / 유창혁 (player 69): Turkish and Ukrainian evidence follow-up

Research date: 2026-10-02. Follow-up to [the preliminary source matrix](kifu-name-liu-changhe-source-matrix-2026-10-02.md). This is a bounded, read-only name investigation, not a candidate approval or a decision about any individual SGF slot.

## Identity and original reading

The [Korea Baduk Association player record](https://www.baduk.or.kr/record/player_view.asp?pkey=10000003) identifies **유창혁(劉昌赫)**, born **1966-04-25**, a Korean 9-dan professional who entered in **1984** and won the Fujitsu, Ing, Samsung, Chunlan, and LG world titles. The [Nihon Ki-in 2019 player profile](https://www.nihonkiin.or.jp/special/wgc/player.html) independently pairs **劉 昌赫**, Japanese phonetic **ユ・チャンヒョク**, and international **Yoo Chang-Hyuk** with the same birth date and career. The Korean spelling, rather than the Chinese reading of 刘昌赫, is the starting point for the unresolved languages. The [National Institute of Korean Language romanization rules](https://www.korean.go.kr/front_eng/roman/roman_01.do) map the Korean letters in 유창혁 to systematic **Yu Changhyeok** (family name first; optional given-name hyphen). This is a pronunciation-based Latin rendering, not proof of Turkish usage. The Japanese kana is an independent reading cue, not Korean audio.

## Unapproved transliteration leads

- **Turkish:** `Yu Changhyeok` is a defensible *Latin-script fallback* from the Korean national romanization rules, with the correct Korean family/given-name order. It is not a demonstrated customary Turkish form. Established international Go sources also write `Yoo Changhyuk` / `Yoo Chang-Hyuk`; selecting between those and the systematic form needs target-language evidence or independent language review. In particular, do not silently turn `ch` into Turkish `ç` or `eo` into a Turkish vowel without a documented convention.
- **Ukrainian:** `Ю Чхан-Хьок` is a **provisional phonetic candidate**, not a witnessed name. The Korean source gives 유 / 창 / 혁. Ukrainian usage for **Хьок** (혁) is visible in a [Ukrainian Wikipedia biography of a *different* Korean person, Чан Хьок](https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A7%D0%B0%D0%BD_%D0%A5%D1%8C%D0%BE%D0%BA), and **Чхан** appears in [Ukrainian Netflix credits](https://www.netflix.com/ua/Title/81397558) for other Korean names. O. M. Shchegel's [2009 Ukrainian linguistic study, pp. 75–86](https://www.inmo.org.ua/assets/files/movoznavstvo/2009_1.pdf) proposes family-name spacing and a hyphen between given-name syllables, while describing Ukrainian Korean-name transcription as unsettled. Those analogies do not establish how Ukrainian Go writers name **this** player; `Ю Чханхьок` without a hyphen and other vowel/spacing choices remain possible. Independent Korean-to-Ukrainian review is required before any candidate is approved.

## Check against the nine preliminary positives

A focused conflict check found no identity conflict with `en`, `cn`, `tw`, `jp`, `ko`, `de`, `es`, `fr`, or `ru` in the preliminary matrix. The [Taiwanese Haifong Go Institute Ing Cup history](https://www.haifong.org/game/classes/8AD0F09CC001B8A2EEBBC376AEEAB539) provides an additional traditional-Chinese **劉昌赫** witness specifically as the 1996 champion, matching the Korean title record. Existing orthographic variation remains: `Yoo Chang-hyuk` versus `Yoo Changhyuk`/`Yoo Chang-Hyuk` in Latin sources; `劉` versus compatibility `劉` in traditional-character sources. These are form variants, not evidence of another person. This was a spot check, not a fresh nine-language source audit.

**Disposition:** `tr` and `ua` remain unresolved. Neither provisional form should be written to production or marked approved on this evidence. No database write, player association, or deployment was performed.
