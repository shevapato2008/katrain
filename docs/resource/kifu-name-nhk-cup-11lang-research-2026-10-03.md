## Identity, edition, and stage grammar

The series identity is well supported: the Japanese official archive names NHK and Nihon Ki-in as organizers and gives a 50-player selected tournament; English CWI and Korean sources describe the same broadcast Go event. Chinese Go news calls the numbered competition `NHK杯快棋赛`, and Haifong records numbered NHK Cup wins. Japanese official records use `第N回` in edition headings and `1回戦`, `2回戦`, and later main-tournament stages. Chinese raw labels contain `第N期` or `第N届`, optional `日本`, and zero or one round phrase. Treat edition as integer `N` and round as a separate integer/stage component; local punctuation and ordinal forms are presentation syntax. Do not bake `日本` into the series title where it is only a source qualifier. Do not infer an album’s exact game or owner from the shared base string.

The launch date is a wording boundary: the Japanese official archive says the series was founded in 1953; the Korean article lists its first winner in 1954; the translated Kifubara page calls it founded in 1954. Record 1953 as the official founding field and 1954 as first-edition/first-winner year where needed. Do not calculate edition or match identity from year alone.

The 647 explicit rows are compatible with a reusable rule: preserve the number in the edition component; map source `期`/`届` to that number rather than to distinct event identities; normalize observed `第1轮`/`第一轮`/`1轮` and equivalents through `3` to a round integer; allow no round, because edition-only rows exist. The source inventory’s holes in editions and rounds are not negative evidence. The single `single_edition_fragment` is held until its album’s SGF date/opponents and event provenance confirm that its reordered `日本第43期...` text is the same event family.
