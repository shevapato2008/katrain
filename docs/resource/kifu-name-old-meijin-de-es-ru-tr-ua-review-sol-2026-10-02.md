# Historical Old Meijin: de/es/ru/tr/ua source review (2026-10-02)

Scope: the Yomiuri-sponsored Japanese Go series of 1962–1975, called **旧・名人戦** in [Nihon Ki-in records](https://www.nihonkiin.or.jp/profile/enkaku/) and **Old Meijin** in the [CWI game archive](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~aeb/go/games/games/OMeijin/). The Asahi-sponsored series starting in 1976 is a different record. These are display-name recommendations for editorial review, not proof of official local-language names.

| Locale | Exact language evidence | Assessment and suggested display |
|---|---|---|
| de | Go education site [Go Magic, German Meijin entry](https://gomagic.org/de/go-term/meijin/) says “die Turniere vor 1976 zählen deshalb heute zur **Old Meijin-Serie**” and heads its Yomiuri list “**Alte Serie (1962–1975, Sponsor Yomiuri)**.” | **Old Meijin-Serie** is directly attested and unambiguous. It is a German-context English loan, not an established name from the German Go federation. Prefer the shorter **Old Meijin** if the UI omits *Serie* for other tournament names; that shorter form is the CWI title, not an independently attested German proper name. The [DGoZ “Der alte Meijin”](https://www.dgob.de/wp-content/uploads/dgoz/pdf/2012/dgoz2012_6_low.pdf) refers to a *current titleholder* and must not be used as evidence for **Alter Meijin**. |
| es | [Spanish Wikipedia, Go Seigen](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_Seigen) labels his 1962 runner-up result “**Meijin antiguo**”; [Hideyuki Fujisawa](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hideyuki_Fujisawa) uses the same table label for 1962 and 1970 wins. [Go Magic, Spanish Meijin entry](https://gomagic.org/es/go-term/meijin/) independently distinguishes “**serie antigua (Old Meijin)**” from “Serie nueva (1976–actualidad, patrocinio de Asahi).” | **Meijin antiguo** is a directly used tournament-table label and a reasonable candidate. The two Wikipedia player entries are not independent naming authorities; Go Magic corroborates the old/new identity but uses a description rather than this exact name. No Spanish federation naming found. |
| ru | A Russian Go club's [Ishida Yoshio book listing](https://nikoraido.ru/books/isida-josio-ocenka-pozicii/) identifies a game as “12-й розыгрыш титула Мэйдзин (**«Старый Мэйдзин»**), 3-я партия матча” and separately lists the “1-й розыгрыш титула Мэйдзин” (new numbering). [Russian Wikipedia's Meijin article](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9C%D1%8D%D0%B9%D0%B4%D0%B7%D0%B8%D0%BD_%28%D0%B3%D0%BE%29) says the 1962–1975 editions “именуются **старый мэйдзин**.” | **Старый Мэйдзин** is the best supported Russian display candidate. The book listing supplies especially clear old/new edition disambiguation. Go Magic's [Russian page](https://gomagic.org/ru/go-term/meijin/) uses “Старая серия” but leaves *Old Meijin* in English, so it is not evidence for a competing proper name. |
| tr | [Go Magic, Turkish Meijin entry](https://gomagic.org/tr/go-term/meijin/) says the pre-1976 tournaments “bugün **Eski Meijin serisi** olarak anılıyor” and heads the Yomiuri list “**Eski seri (1962–1975, Yomiuri sponsorluğunda)**.” | **Eski Meijin serisi** is directly attested in a Turkish Go source and distinguishes the historical series. It remains a single-site localized usage, not a documented Turkish federation convention. If the product convention omits “series,” **Eski Meijin** is an editorial shortening of the quoted phrase and should be marked as such. |
| ua | No Ukrainian source found naming the Japanese Yomiuri series. The [Ukrainian Go Federation](https://ufgo.org/opys-ta-pravyla-ukrajinskoji-onlajn-lihy-ho/) does use “**Український онлайн Мейдзин**,” proving Ukrainian Go-context spelling **Мейдзин**, but that is a *different domestic title*. A defensible **editorial provenance label** is **Мейдзин (Йоміурі, 1962–1975)**: use the federation's spelling of *Meijin*, Ukrainian *Yomiuri*, and the CWI period/sponsor boundary. Do not present this composite as an established Ukrainian tournament name. **Старий Мейдзин** is a possible literal translation but is unattested for this series and could also read as an old titleholder. |

## Evidence limits and application rule

Go Magic's de/es/ru/tr entries are versions of one multilingual article, not four independent attestations; its precise local wording is useful for display, but cannot establish broad regional convention. Wikipedia player tables likewise demonstrate usage, not official naming. The Russian book listing is stronger tournament-specific evidence because it explicitly contrasts old and new numbering.

Use a locale-specific historical label only when its cited passage actually points to the Yomiuri series. Bare **Meijin**, descriptions of an elderly Meijin, Kawabata's *The Master of Go*, the Edo-era rank, and the Ukrainian online title are different referents. For `ua`, retain the provenance in the display string until a direct Ukrainian source or editorial localization review supports a shorter conventional name.
