# Japanese Go Oza: Ukrainian display-name editorial decision

Date: 2026-10-02. Independent follow-up to the [11-language source memo](kifu-name-oza-11lang-source-memo-2026-10-02.md) and [Sol's es/fr/tr/ua source review](kifu-name-oza-es-fr-tr-ua-review-sol-2026-10-02.md).

**Decision: select `Одза` as the Ukrainian (`ua`) display form, with evidence class `generated`.** This is an editorial decision about the spelling only. Direct Ukrainian usage for this exact professional Go series remains unestablished. Any candidate or import remains pending its normal evidence and approval process; this memo does not approve a registry entry, bundle, staging operation, or database write.

The requested alternative `Oza` retains the organizer's English spelling, but does not satisfy the stated Cyrillic requirement for a Ukrainian generated display. There is sufficient Ukrainian linguistic evidence to choose `Одза` without changing that requirement or treating Russian usage as Ukrainian evidence.

## Identity and derivation

| Point | Verified source and consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Exact series | The [Nihon Ki-in Japanese page](https://www.nihonkiin.or.jp/match/oza/062.html) identifies 王座戦, the three organizers (Nikkei, Nihon Ki-in, Kansai Ki-in), and the five-game title match. Its [English archive](https://archive.nihonkiin.or.jp/match/oza/index-e.html) calls the same series **Oza Title**. These anchors identify the Japanese professional Go event. |
| Japanese reading | [Shogakukan's Digital Daijisen entry on Kotobank](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%8E%8B%E5%BA%A7-448906) gives 王座 the reading **おうざ**. This dictionary entry supports the reading only: its Go/shogi title sense does not distinguish the two competitions. |
| Ukrainian consonant | V. A. Rubel's Ukrainian paper, [*Transcription of Japanese words by means of the Ukrainian language: problems and proposals* (2009)](https://jnas.nbuv.gov.ua/uk/article/UJRN-0000338274), explicitly accepts **дза** for ざ/ザ in the discussion on printed pp. 152–153 and in the proposed Ukrainian Table 3 on p. 156. The [original six-page PDF](https://jnas.nbuv.gov.ua/j-pdf/SkhS_2009_4_20.pdf) was downloaded and visually checked. Table 2 is a separate Russian comparison and is not the rule used here. |
| Consistent Ukrainian scheme and long vowel | [T. S. Dementieva and T. K. Komarnytska (2024), Tables 2–3, printed pp. 241–242](https://philol.vernadskyjournals.in.ua/journals/2024/6_2024/40.pdf#page=6), compare Ukrainian transcription systems. Their Kovalenko column gives **дза** for the first syllable in the ザ row. It records macrons for long vowels in specialist texts, but omits the length of /а, у, о, ю/ in translations. Thus a plain display can use **о** for the long initial vowel. This is a scholarly account of a named system, not evidence of an official Ukrainian tournament name. |

Applying that documented practical-transcription choice to the Japanese title stem gives **おうざ → о + дза → Одза**. The generated result is an editorial inference from the reading and the Ukrainian rules; none of the linguistic sources names this Go tournament `Одза`.

This choice uses the short proper-name stem, corresponding to the organizer's `Oza`, rather than adding a newly translated generic descriptor. It does not attempt a character-by-character rendering of the complete Japanese string 王座戦.

## Alternatives and evidence limits

- **`Oza`:** retain as the English/Latin source name if needed; do not use it as this Cyrillic Ukrainian generated display.
- **`Оза`:** a spelling based on an intervocalic realization of Japanese /z/ is conceivable under a different phonetic approach. It is not the result of the documented mapping selected above, and the reviewed sources do not establish it as this tournament's Ukrainian name.
- **`Оодза` / `О̄дза`:** these would explicitly represent vowel length. The ordinary display convention selected here omits that mark; the Japanese reading remains available in the evidence.
- **`титул Одза`:** a possible descriptive phrase, but the sources reviewed do not establish it as a conventional Ukrainian series name. Use the minimal stem `Одза`.

Sol's exclusions remain valid: Russian text on a Ukrainian site does not attest Ukrainian naming, and invitations to the World Students Go Oza Championship identify a different event. The shared spelling of Russian and generated Ukrainian `Одза` does not merge their evidence. The shogi 王座戦 is also outside this decision.

The supplementary search covered Ukrainian transcription scholarship and exact/contextual `Одза` and `Оза` with Go/Japan terms, including Ukrainian federation and encyclopedia results. It established a rule basis, not direct Ukrainian usage or exhaustive absence of such usage. Multiple Ukrainian transcription approaches exist; this memo makes a documented editorial selection and does not claim national linguistic consensus.

**Handoff:** Ukrainian spelling decision resolved as `Одза`; provenance remains `generated`; conventional/attested status remains unsupported. Future controlled import must retain the Japanese professional Go identity and independently satisfy its required source capture and approval gates. Only this memo was authored for this task; no code, database, staging, or commit operation was performed.
