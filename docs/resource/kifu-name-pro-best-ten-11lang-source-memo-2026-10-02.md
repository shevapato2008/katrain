# CWI “Pro Best Ten” 11-language source memo — 2026-10-02

## Identity finding

The event represented by CWI’s `ProBest10` is **Asahi Pro Best Ten / プロ十傑戦**, a Japanese professional Go tournament sponsored by Asahi Shimbun, with 12 title-result editions in 1964–1975. First-edition play is indexed in 1963; a Nihon Ki-in timeline also lists a 1962 start without explaining the date. See the [independent follow-up](kifu-name-pro-best-ten-uncertain-sol-2026-10-02.md) before assigning a founding year. It ended when Asahi took over sponsorship of the new Meijin tournament in 1976. It is a distinct event series, not a generic ranking artifact.

CWI’s page title is “Asahi Pro Best Ten title results.” It explicitly gives the Japanese name プロ十傑戦, sponsor, dates, final-game format, winners, and links to each edition’s final games. The CWI landing page exposes 36 title-final SGFs (2/3 games for editions 1–6 and 3–5 for editions 7–12); this is narrower than the supplied full event-group artifact of about 1,171 games, which presumably includes qualifying, ranking, and placement games. Don’t describe the CWI landing page itself as containing all ~1,171 games.

The Japanese Nihon Ki-in publication calls the competition プロ十傑戦 and describes the initial qualification concept: amateur 十傑戦 regional qualifiers voted for professionals, the top four were seeded into the final eight, and other eligible players plus four qualifiers made a knockout field. This context explains “Best Ten”: the event also produced a top-ten ranking, rather than being a simple ten-player title bracket. A Chinese-language Go article independently uses 专业十杰战, gives Asahi as organizer, says there were 12 editions, and links its end to the new Meijin event. Its 1963 start may refer to first-edition play; do not treat it as a proved error.

### Distinguish nearby series

- **All Japan #1 / Zen Nihon Dai-ichi** is separate. The Dai-ichi source groups it under “All Japan First Place,” with editions beginning in 1970, distinct from its earlier Nihon Ki-in Dai-ichi strand. It shares some players and years with Pro Best Ten but has separate naming, results, and continuity. Never merge these records by the word “#1” or “best.”
- The 1976 Asahi Meijin tournament is the successor in sponsorship/history context, but it is a separately named competition with numbering restarted. Preserve Pro Best Ten as its own event group.
- Do not confuse it with Amateur Best Ten / アマ十傑戦, Student Go Best Ten, or modern generic “top ten” lists.

## Source record and bounded search

1. CWI Go game archive, primary event/group page: https://homepages.cwi.nl/~aeb/go/games/games/ProBest10/ — directly attests “Asahi Pro Best Ten (プロ十傑戦),” Asahi Shimbun sponsorship, 1964–1975, 12 annual editions, and title-game links.
2. Nihon Ki-in’s official Go information service, Kido Web: https://note.com/nihonkiin/n/n666233560d94 — Japanese exact name プロ十傑戦; historical description of the voting/seeding format. The page is paywalled after the opening historical section, but the exact name and relevant format context are visible.
3. Sina Sports Go feature: https://k.sina.cn/article_1443981667_5611696300100vaqo.html — simplified Chinese exact phrase 专业十杰战, Asahi sponsorship and end upon new Meijin takeover; its “began in 1963” can coexist with 1964 title results, as the follow-up explains.
4. CWI separate Dai-ichi results lineage: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dai-ichi_(Go_competition) — secondary source labels a distinct Zen Nihon Dai-ichi “All Japan First Place” strand and lists its 1970–1975 events. CWI’s own tournament/results index is cited in that page’s references: https://homepages.cwi.nl/~aeb/go/games/games/NihonKiinChampionship/ . Use this only to disambiguate the English “All Japan #1” label, not as primary event identity evidence.
5. Academic catalog record listing the Japanese bibliographic title 朝日新聞社プロ十傑戦: https://ouc.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/1120/files/Text.pdf (search within for “朝日新聞社プロ十傑戦”).
6. Chinese-language Meijin history page: https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/名人战_(日本围棋) — describes the 1976 transition from 職業十傑戰 to the new 名人戰 and distinguishes lineage/numbering. Secondary corroboration only.

Bounded negative searches: queried CWI/event name plus French/German/Spanish; Korean phrase and English alias; and Chinese/Go terms. The independent follow-up found a Korean federation name. Reliable translated DE/ES/FR/RU/TR/UA titles were not located; absence here is not proof that no local source exists. Chinese exact evidence located is “专业十杰战”; Traditional wording above is a script conversion proposal, not a discovered Taiwanese attestation.

## Recommendation to parent

Use the event identity and 1964–1975 dates with high confidence. Preserve a stable English alias in languages without native-source evidence. Treat CN’s “朝日职业十杰战” and JP’s sponsor-qualified form as editorially generated labels, with source-backed alternatives recorded above. Escalate only if product policy requires exact native-source attestation for all locales; reliable public attestations were not found for six locales despite bounded queries.
