## Series identity

The [CWI Pro Best Ten results](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~aeb/go/games/games/ProBest10/) call the event `Asahi Pro Best Ten (プロ十傑戦)`, name Asahi Shimbun, and enumerate 12 title-result editions in 1964–1975, including Cho Chikun's 12th win. The [Nihon Ki-in's Kido Web article](https://note.com/nihonkiin/n/n666233560d94), visible opening, calls it `プロ十傑戦` and describes professional selection through amateur voting. The Asahi amateur 十傑 event, the later Asahi Meijin, and other “best ten” competitions are separate. The 1962 Nihon Ki-in timeline entry, indexed 1963 first-edition play, and 1964 first title result remain distinct date facts; this name review does not resolve a founding year.

The [CWI Championship results](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~aeb/go/games/games/NKCh/) call the second event `Nihon Ki-in Championship (日本棋院選手権戦)`, enumerate editions 1–22 with title-result years 1954–1975, and identify Tengen as successor. [Nihon Ki-in's historical record](https://archive.nihonkiin.or.jp/match/docs/5renpa_20100825.pdf), page 1, names `日本棋院選手権戦` separately from `日本棋院第一位決定戦`, `全日本第一位決定戦`, and `天元戦`. The Kansai Ki-in Championship and Kansai Ki-in first-place event also remain separate. `Nihon Ki-in` alone is the association, not this series.
