## Identity anchor and orthography

The Nihon Ki-in's Japanese professional profile identifies **張 栩**, gives the reading **チョウ ウ** (romanized by that source as `CHANG, Hsu`), says he was born in Taipei, Taiwan, lists Tokyo headquarters membership, and records his professional rank as 九段. It further says he entered as 1-dan in 1994 and reached 9-dan in 2003. [Official Nihon Ki-in profile](https://www.nihonkiin.or.jp/player/htm/ki000331.htm), actual page language Japanese; body passage: `張 栩（チョウ ウ / CHANG, Hsu）` and `台湾台北市出身 ... 日本棋院東京本院所属`.

Independent identity corroboration: Taiwan's official Presidential Office report calls him `旅日職業圍棋國手張栩` and identifies him as the 58th Honinbo titleholder who reached 9-dan after winning that title. [ROC Presidential Office](https://www.president.gov.tw/NEWS/7749), actual page language traditional Chinese. Haifong Go Institute's traditional-Chinese interview headline/body names `張栩` and describes his seven-title grand slam/five-crown period. [Haifong Go Institute interview](https://haifong.org/news/content/F05540B657DC16C1402AFFFCCCC72F54), actual page language traditional Chinese. These sources support the player identity and traditional form. Simplified `张栩` is a distinct witnessed form in the mainland source below, not a name inferred by conversion.

Rank and title remain separate attributes: current professional rank in the Nihon Ki-in profile is 9-dan; title names such as Honinbo, Kisei, Oza, and Meijin are competition titles, not part of the personal name. Historical source pages may append a historical rank/title and should not be copied as the display name.
