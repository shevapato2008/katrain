## Editorial decision

Prefer **`Cho Chikun`** as the Spanish display form for the Japanese-affiliated Go professional represented by `趙治勲` / `조치훈`. Retain **`Cho Chihun`** as a sourced search alias if the separate alias workflow is later reviewed. These are two romanized forms for one person in the checked material, not evidence of distinct players. The old matrix's Spanish conflict is real and must remain visible in any new research/candidate record; an existing `Cho Chihun` field must not silently be treated as `Cho Chikun`.

The fixed [Spanish Wikipedia revision 174242434](https://es.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?oldid=174242434) is internally mixed: its article title and URL are `Cho Chikun`, while its infobox `nombre`, lead, and a note explaining Korean versus Japanese pronunciation say `Cho Chihun`. The original main-slot wikitext SHA-256 `1f9d9e4c3592bd7ed50bf5404400952303a66efead28b6009d1f817d1a0a4092` matches the earlier matrix; the separately captured parse API reports `revid=174242434`. Neither spelling can be dismissed as a search-index error or a second identity.

The stronger Spanish Go usage in this bounded choice favors `Cho Chikun`. The [Asociación Argentina del Juego de Go's Spanish tournament account](https://go.org.ar/informacion-y-noticias/105-relatos/190-mi-participacion-en-el-mundial-de-go-oza-2) uses that form twice in professional-match context. [Club de Go de Madrid's Spanish library entry](https://clubgomadrid.org/library?hl=es) lists `Autor: Cho Chikun` and calls him a Go master in its Spanish description. The [Asociación Colombiana de Go's learning page](https://www.colombiago.org/index.php/aprende/como-jugar) independently credits `Problemas Elementales - Cho Chikun`, though that link label supplies little biographical detail. These are three publishers, not three professional biographies; the Argentine prose carries the clearest contextual name use. The Wikipedia title agrees with them, but its body supports the alias.

Identity was cross-checked outside those Spanish pages. The [Nihon Ki-in player profile](https://www.nihonkiin.or.jp/player/htm/ki000004_2.html) gives `趙　治勲（チョウ　チクン / CHO, Chi Hun）`, 9 dan, 1956-06-20, Busan origin, and Japanese association. The independently published [Korea Baduk Association profile](https://www.baduk.or.kr/record/player_view.asp?pkey=20000020) gives `조치훈(趙治勳)`, the same birth date, 9 dan, and Japan affiliation. The Japanese/Korean Hanja glyph difference is the expected source-script form; the shared name, date, profession, and affiliation establish the person. Nihon Ki-in's Latin `CHO, Chi Hun` explains why the `Chihun` variant is plausible, but is not itself Spanish usage.
